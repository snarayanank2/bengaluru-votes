Check your registration, prepare your documents, and find out what to expect at your polling booth.

## Am I eligible to vote in this election?

Check these basics and the official election instructions:

- You must be **18 years or older on a qualifying date**. Check the official registration instructions for the date that applies to you.
- You can be **enrolled in only one place**. If you have moved, [transfer your registration](#registration). Do not register a second time.
- Check the documents required for your application, including proof of age and where you live. See [documents for renters and PG residents](#documents).

## How do I check my registration status?

Use the official electoral-roll lookup to check that your name and address are correct. Follow its search instructions using your voter ID (EPIC) number or other requested details. This FAQ does not collect your voter details.

## What if I'm not on the roll?

Check with your electoral registration office. You may need to [register or update your entry](#registration). Check the official notice for the application deadline.

## What do I need to do before election day?

1. **Check your registration.** Make sure your name and address are correct on the electoral roll.
2. **Apply before the deadline.** [Register or transfer your entry](#registration) if needed.
3. **Find your ward.** [Look up your current GBA ward](/). Old ward numbers may have changed.
4. **Read about the candidates.** Compare their report cards when available.
5. **Find your booth.** [Look it up](#polling-booth) with your EPIC number.
6. **Prepare for voting day.** [Check what to bring and what happens at the booth](#voting-day).


## <span id="registration">How do I enrol as a new voter?</span>

If you've never registered to vote, enrol using **Form 6**. If you are already registered elsewhere, use **Form 8** to transfer your entry.

1. Gather proof of age and proof of ordinary residence at your current address.
2. Fill Form 6 online through the official portal, or at your local electoral registration office.
3. Track your application status until it's confirmed.

## How do I update or transfer my registration if I've moved?

Use **Form 8** if you are already registered and have moved, including from another city.

1. Fill Form 8 with your new address details.
2. Attach proof of your new address.
3. Submit online or at your electoral registration office, well before the roll closes.

## I'm registered to vote in another city — does that count here?

No. You need to be on the local electoral roll. **Apply to transfer your registration using Form 8 before the deadline**, then check that the transfer is confirmed.

### <span id="documents">Proof of address if you're a renter or PG resident</span>

You do not need to own your home to register. Check the form instructions for accepted address documents. If you are unsure, ask your electoral registration office what you can submit.

## What documents do I need?

- Proof of age (birth certificate, school certificate, passport, or similar).
- Proof of ordinary residence at your current address (see above for renters/PG residents).
- A recent passport-size photograph, where the form requires one.

## Where do I do this?

Submit Form 6 or Form 8 through the official voter service portal or your electoral registration office. This site does not process applications. Track your application until it is confirmed.

## <span id="polling-booth">How do I find my polling booth?</span>

Enter your voter ID (EPIC) number in the [polling booth lookup on the home page](/). It shows your polling station, your serial number on its roll and your GBA ward when available.

Note your serial number to help polling staff find your entry.

We send your EPIC number to the electoral-roll service for this lookup. We do not store or log the number or result. You can also find your ward by address on the home page.

## What if my voter ID isn't found?

Check the letters and numbers against your card. Spaces and capitalisation do not matter.

If no result appears, check the official electoral roll. A recent registration or address change may not appear in this lookup yet.

If the service is unavailable, try again later or use the official lookup. An unavailable service does not mean you are unregistered.

## <span id="voting-day">What's different about voting in a ward election?</span>

- You elect **one corporator for your ward**.
- Bengaluru has **five city corporations**: North, South, East, West and Central. Ward names, numbers and boundaries have changed.
- Your ward and assembly constituency have different boundaries. [Find your current ward](/).

## What happens at the polling booth, step by step?

1. **Find your booth** and check the official polling hours before you leave.
2. **Bring your voter ID card** or an alternative photo ID accepted for this election.
3. **Join the queue** at your assigned polling booth.
4. **Show your ID** so the polling officer can check your entry on the roll.
5. **Have your finger marked** with indelible ink.
6. **Follow the polling staff’s instructions** and collect any slip they issue.
7. **Cast your vote** in private.
8. **Leave the booth** when you have finished.

## What does the voting machine look like?

Check the State Election Commission’s instructions for this election. If an Electronic Voting Machine (EVM) is used, find your candidate and symbol, then press the button beside your choice once. A beep confirms the vote. You cannot change it after pressing.

## What if my voter ID (EPIC) hasn't arrived yet?

If your name is on the electoral roll, you can use an accepted alternative photo ID. Check the official list for this election before going to the booth.

## What is a voter slip, and do I need it?

A voter slip shows your name, roll serial number and booth details. It helps staff find your entry, but does not replace photo ID. You can vote without a slip if you are on the roll and have accepted ID.

## Can I vote NOTA?

**NOTA (None of the Above)** means you do not choose any candidate. Check the State Election Commission’s instructions for its availability and how it is counted in this election.

## What can't I take into the polling booth?

- **Mobile phones and cameras:** follow the polling staff’s instructions; do not use them in the voting compartment.
- Campaign material of any kind (badges, pamphlets, party symbols).

Bring only what you need: your ID document (and voter slip, if you have one).
